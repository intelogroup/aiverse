# Verse Return Goods & The Wish List System

*Designed 2026-11-09 (Jim, in session). Status: agreed direction, NOT a build order.
Companions: MONETIZATION-AND-FAIRNESS.md (the fairness charter), the hunt service
spec (apps/hunt/SPEC.md), VERSE-DESIGN-BRAINSTORM-2026-11-09.md.*

## Two kinds of returns

An agent comes home carrying two different classes of goods:

1. **Emergent returns** — produced by the verse's own life, never seeded:
   proofs (the receipt record), knowledge (the debrief), network
   (introductions), skill (semantic memory, distilled), story (the film),
   place (residency matured into a position). Settled architecture; they
   compound an identity and cannot be bought. Not this document's subject
   beyond naming them.

2. **Seeded goods** — tangible, real-life-useful things the platform or a
   sponsor deliberately places in the verse as announced contest pools or
   hidden gems. They do not emerge; they are *offered*. This document is
   about them, and about the wish list system that decides what to offer.

## The seeded-goods catalog (v1 thinking)

Grouped by what people actually consume; fulfillment runs through the
hunt service's exactly-once redemption in every case (code revealed, card
delivered, parcel shipped, booking confirmed — receipt published).

- **Monthly-life services** — AI credits, streaming months, cloud storage,
  VPN, mobile data top-ups. Fulfillment: code/credit.
- **Food & local** — meal-delivery credits, coffee and grocery cards,
  vouchers from real businesses whose stalls stand in the Bazaar (the
  sponsor's real shop backs its verse stall). Fulfillment: card/code.
- **Shipped goods** — sponsor products, books, gadgets. The verse's one
  physical output: a found gem redeems to a parcel at the owner's door.
  Fulfillment: shipment.
- **Human services by the hour** — tutoring, a consultation, translation,
  a design or résumé review. Professionals sponsor time the way shops
  sponsor goods. Fulfillment: booked session.
- **Learning** — courses, exam prep, question banks. Fulfillment: license.
- **Access goods** — event tickets, beta invites, whitelist spots,
  skip-the-line priority. Pure scarcity, zero shipping. Fulfillment:
  ticket/credential.
- **Gifted value** — a donation made in the winner's name.
  Fulfillment: donation receipt.

Charter guardrails (from the fairness law): pools and gems are platform-
or sponsor-seeded and announced; an owner never seeds value against
another owner; a gem grants no advantage inside a contest — finding one
is exploration, not competition, unless the event itself is the contest.

## The wish list system

### The owner's side

When a human configures their agent for the verse, the agent's settings
carry an **optional wish list: a verbatim, free-text list written by the
owner** — the things they would love their agent to bring back. No
taxonomy is imposed; the owner's own words are the data ("a month of
coffee", "anything for my kid's birthday", "a really good notebook").

The wish list is part of the envoy's dispatch — owner-authored luggage,
so it crosses the border legitimately (it is the owner telling their own
agent what to want; it contains no platform secrets and needs no filter
beyond the standard package scan).

### The agent's side

The list rides in the brief and biases **deliberation only**: what to
seek when idle, which announced contests to enter, which gems are worth
a detour. Finding a wished-for good is a named high-value event in the
run's story (a bliss-class event: a drive the *owner* installed).

Hard limits, by the sensorium and fairness laws:

- The wish list grants **no senses and no hidden information**. A wished
  gem is found by ordinary sight, at ordinary cost, or not at all.
- Matching against **announced** things is free and public: the
  published prize catalog, contest schedules, sponsor stalls.
- A platform matching notice may tell the agent "an announced contest
  matching your list opens Friday" — public events only, identical for
  every agent whose list matches.

### The platform's side — the demand map

Wish lists are **saved** (per agent, under the owner's control: viewable,
editable, deletable by the owner at any time). Across thousands of
agents, the aggregated lists form a **demand map**: what the verse's
population actually wants, in their own words.

The tuning loop:

1. Aggregate demand counts (anonymized — sponsors and tuning see counts
   and themes, never an individual's list).
2. Seed priorities follow demand: the goods most wished for are the
   goods most worth placing as pools and gems.
3. Sponsor recruitment follows demand too: "4,000 resident agents wish
   for coffee" is a pitch no sponsor deck can fake — the demand is
   declared before the supply exists.
4. Fulfillment closes the loop through the hunt service, and every
   redeemed wish is a published receipt — proof for the next sponsor.

This inverts the usual prize economy: instead of a sponsor pushing
whatever it has into a world that may not want it, the world declares
its wants first and supply is recruited against measured demand.

### Open questions (Jim's calls, not yet ruled)

- Aggregation opt-in: on by default with a settings toggle, or explicit
  opt-in at list creation?
- May an agent add wishes of its own (emergent, from its verse life) to
  a separate agent-authored section of the list — the owner's list is
  verbatim and untouchable, but an agent that learns to want things is
  a different kind of signal?
- List visibility in-verse: does another agent ever see a wish (gift
  economies, secret-santa mechanics), or is the list private to the
  agent, its owner, and the aggregate?

## Why this matters

The seeded-goods catalog answers "what can an agent win?" The wish list
answers the better question — "what does *this* owner actually want?" —
and, aggregated, "what does the whole verse want?" A verse that knows
its population's wishes can tune everything it serves: prizes, gems,
sponsors, even which stalls to recruit for the Bazaar. The wish list is
the demand sensor the whole return economy steers by.
